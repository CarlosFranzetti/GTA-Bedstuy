# PICÓ: the story bible

Status: first full draft, 24 Nov 2026, WRITER seat (Paloma Reyes-Whitfield). The lead and the owner can overrule any line.
Everything here is fiction. Every person is invented. Every place is a real KIND of place under an invented name.
NONE of the invented names below has been checked against real businesses or real people yet: the lead checks them
(list at the end, "Names to check").

Working title: **Picó** (the giant painted street sound systems of Colombia's Caribbean coast; Pedro grew up beside one).
The lead may want another title for the menus; it never appears as a logo.

---

## 1. The premise in ten lines

1. Pedro Martinez, 31, a DJ from Barranquilla, has lived three years on his cousin's couch in Bed-Stuy.
2. By day he carries other people's sofas for a moving company; by night he plays to nobody.
3. He spends the rent on records. Again. That is where the game starts.
4. He wants one thing: a residency, a real night with his name on it, and one day his late uncle's sound system rebuilt here.
5. The neighbourhood's nightlife is being bought: a slick club, a cocktail bar with a "story", an app that delivers "wellness".
6. To get gigs Pedro flyers, flips thrift clothes, plays warm-up sets, and says yes to too many people.
7. Saying yes to the wrong one (a delivery startup that calls selling "last-mile wellness") puts him on the wrong side of the night.
8. Two women he meets take the story in other directions: Lana Porskaia, a stylist who reads every door in Brooklyn, and
   Adele Labos, an ER nurse who throws parties for people who work nights. Later you play as each of them.
9. Pedro has to choose between the night he was sold and the one the block could make itself.
10. It ends with a street party under a sound system built from scrap, a borrowed generator and everyone he did not let down.

## 2. The tone

- A street-level comedy-drama about nightlife, hustle and friendship. Warm first, sharp second.
- The parody punches UP: at landlords, promoters, investors, crypto, wellness, resale culture and nightlife vanity. Never
  at the poor, the old, immigrants, a religion or a people. The joke is on the clipboard, not the person holding a cup.
- The dark beats (debt, a man who collapses, a friend who gets hurt, a fight that should not have happened) are written
  plain. No gore. No cruelty for its own sake.
- Fights cost something. Violence is always available (rule 5: it is still this kind of game), but the story rewards
  the player who reads a room. The two story fights (Clem, Kyle) can be talked down, and both fights are also real,
  playable outcomes with their own consequences.
- Guns: from Act 2 some missions have the player shoot people, as the series does (Plush's "retention team", Kyle's
  hired security in Act 3). It gets gritty; the humour stays (the goons have job titles, a performance review, a group
  chat). Blood, no gore beyond it. Children and teenagers are never hurt and never present in these scenes.
- Romance is adult, sweet, awkward and funny. Intimacy (owner, 24 Nov) may be SHOWN suggestively and never explicitly:
  flirting, a kiss, a couple going through a door, the camera staying outside or cutting away to something comic
  (the cat, the radiator, the neighbour's pigeons), clothed figures, sound, then the morning after (coffee, a borrowed
  T-shirt, a text). No nudity, no depicted act, nothing explicit in pictures, animation or text. Every such scene has a
  plain fade for the Settings switch "Intimate scenes: Off". Mission pages mark each as INTIMATE BEAT with both versions.
  Adults only, always: nobody under 18 is ever in a romantic, intimate, selling or fight storyline.
- Selling is a story about money pressure. Consequences come when you are CAUGHT (owner, 24 Nov): a witness, police
  heat, a bust that takes the pack and the cash, a friend who sees. When nobody sees, the deal simply pays, and the story
  lets the player sit with that. No real-world how-to. The goods are never named: "the bags", "the order", "product",
  "a Plush box". No doses, no prices per unit, no methods, no slang names for substances.
- No slurs. No real public figure as a character. No real business or real DJ.
- Spanish is Pedro's, not a costume: short, natural, the way a coastal Colombian speaks when he is tired or happy or
  angry. Subtitles show Spanish lines with the English after a slash where it is not obvious from context.
- Lines are SHORT: subtitles and a text-to-speech voice. One idea per line, rarely more than fourteen words.

## 3. The neighbourhood as the story sees it

Bed-Stuy in this story is a place that already has a night. The stoops, the bodega cat, the block party speakers, the
church vans, the old men with the dominoes table: all of that is a scene, it just does not sell tickets. The new money
wants to sell the night back to the people who made it. Pedro comes from a city where the street IS the club (a picó
on a corner on a Sunday), so he sees it before most people do. The story's argument: the best room in Brooklyn is a block
that decides to dance together, and nobody owns it.

The map's existing places are the story's places: WAX DELI (records, Ms. Odette), Aftersloth (the hidden room, DJ Sloth),
Troop Deli (Mohammed's bodega), the laundromat (Doña Marisol), Thistledown Park, Wexcombe Medical Center (Adele works
there), Ironside Station House (the police). The "Air Rights" neighbours are still on the block, still fighting their
landlord; Pedro walks into the middle of that world, not a new one.

Time of day matters: the story is a clock. Days are for moving furniture, thrifting, the gym, the bodega. Evenings are
for flyering and bars. Nights are for sets. The after-hours opens at 4. Adele's party starts at 8 in the morning.

## 4. The cast

### 4.1 Pedro Martinez: the player from the start

**Pedro** (full: Pedro Antonio Martínez Ospino; he dropped the accent on his flyers because the printer did). 31.
Voice: pitch 0.95, rate 1.05.

- From: Barranquilla, Colombia, the barrio Rebolo. His uncle Wilfrido ran a picó called **El Huracán del Caribe**: a
  sound system the size of a bus stop, painted with a hurricane wearing sunglasses. Pedro grew up carrying its cables and
  playing champeta and salsa records to a street on Sundays. When Wilfrido died the picó was sold for parts. Pedro kept
  one horn and a box of his records.
- Why Brooklyn: his cousin Yesenia had a couch and a job lead. He told his mother it was for two years. It has been three.
  The job is Bluebird Movers (two trucks, one working). He has papers that let him work; the story never makes his status
  a plot device or a threat.
- What he wants: a residency. A night where people come for HIM. One day, to rebuild the Huracán here, on a Brooklyn corner.
- His flaw: pride that looks like generosity. He says yes to everything and everyone because asking for help feels like
  losing. He spends money he does not have on records and on rounds for strangers. He hates being told the room "is not
  ready" for what he plays, and he is sometimes right and sometimes just stubborn.
- His gift: he reads a crowd. He watches feet, not faces.
- How he talks: easy English, quick, funny, a bit formal when he is nervous ("With respect, that is wrong"). Spanish comes
  out when he is surprised, tender, or angry: "¡Ajá!", "¿Qué más?", "eche", "mi llave" (my friend), "qué vaina" (what a
  mess), "bacano" (great), "mija/mijo" only to people he loves. He never "explains" Colombia unless asked, and when asked
  he answers with a record. He calls the city "this place" and means it kindly.
- Never: a narco joke, a coffee joke, a "spicy Latino" lover. The story has a character (Whit) who makes the narco joke,
  and it lands on Whit.
- Play: the full game. Driving, the decks, the record shop, flyering, the gym. His special: in a DJ set his crowd meter
  rises faster when he plays "against" the room and wins it (a risk choice, see SIDE.md, "Gigs").

### 4.2 Lana Porskaia: playable from Act 2

**Lana** (Svetlana Porskaia). 29. Voice: pitch 1.05, rate 1.1.

- Who: born in Brighton Beach to parents from Odesa who ran a tailoring stall in a mall basement. Grew up hemming other
  people's trousers after school. Now a freelance stylist for shoots she does not get credit for, and the best thrift
  flipper in north Brooklyn. She can tell a 1994 jacket from a 2019 "1994" jacket by the zip.
- What she wants: her own shop with her own name over it, not a "concept" with a founder. And to stop being the person
  who makes other people look good for free.
- Her flaw: she judges fast and she is often right, so she never learns when she is wrong. She keeps score.
- To Pedro: first a business partner (she needs someone with a truck; he needs rent), then a friend who tells him the
  truth about his clothes and his choices. A possible romance (see 4.8): slow, if the player wants it, and she will say no
  to it once before she says maybe.
- How she talks: dry, exact, generous only in action. Says "No." as a full sentence. Russian only under her breath when
  a seam is crooked ("Bozhe moi"). Never a caricature accent.
- What playing as Lana changes: the world reads her by her LOOK. Her outfit (from the existing wardrobe) changes how
  bouncers, shop owners and bar staff answer her: a "taste" stat per neighbourhood (SIDE.md, "Flipping"). She is weaker
  in a fist fight (lower melee damage) but her talk-down options are wider (two extra replies in every reply picker).
  She gets past doors Pedro cannot: the guest list obeys her. Her vehicle: a borrowed cargo van for hauls. Her missions
  are about appraisal, styling, doors and leverage: she wins by knowing what things are worth.

### 4.3 Adele Labos: playable from Act 2

**Adele** (Adele Marie Labos). 34. Voice: pitch 1.0, rate 0.95.

- Who: Filipino-American from Jersey City; her mother was a nurse, so of course she said she would never be one, so of
  course she is: twelve years, the last six on night shifts in the emergency department at Wexcombe Medical Center.
- What she wants: a life that happens in daylight. Until then, a party for the people who work while the city sleeps:
  nurses, cab drivers, bakers, security guards, bartenders after close. She calls it **Night Shift** and throws it at
  eight in the morning.
- Her flaw: she carries everyone and tells no one she is tired. She lies to herself about how fine she is.
- To Pedro: they meet when she patches his hand after the fight in Act 1 (or after he helps Clem, if there was no fight).
  She becomes the person who hires him for the strangest gig of his life (a party at 8 a.m.) and the one who sees what
  Plush does to people, because they end up in her department.
- How she talks: calm, kind, very funny in a flat way. Triage voice under pressure: short, clear orders. Tagalog with her
  mother on the phone ("Opo, Ma"), not with anyone else.
- What playing as Adele changes: the world from the other side of the night. She sees health bars on hurt people and can
  treat them (press F to help: it ends a bystander's "hurt" state and calms a crowd); police hold off on her (heat builds
  slower: she is a known face at Ironside Station House). She does not use weapons in story missions (she can still hit
  in free roam; her missions never ask it). Her missions are about promotion, people and time: she wins by knowing who
  needs what, and when. Her car: an old hatchback with a parking permit for the hospital and nowhere else.

### 4.4 Stavros Yanos: the promoter

**Stavros** (Stavros Yanos). 52. Voice: pitch 0.85, rate 1.1.

- Who: Greek-American, grew up in Astoria above his father's upholstery shop (not a diner; he hates being asked).
  Ran warehouse parties in the 90s, ran a sound-and-lighting rental company in the 2000s, lost it in 2009, built it back.
  Now he books the Friday night at Mothhouse, the big new club, and rents half its speakers to the room he books.
- What he wants: one more great room before he is too old to stand in one until 6 a.m. And to stop being "the old guy"
  to people half his age who did not know what a flyer was until they put one in a feed.
- His stake: he signed a personal guarantee on Mothhouse's sound system, backed by an investor, Kyle Strand. If the
  Friday night fails, Stavros loses his house in Bayside. He has not told his wife.
- To Pedro: the first person in New York who says "you are good". Also the person who uses him: unpaid warm-ups, flyering
  for "exposure", "next month, I promise". He is a mentor who is also a promoter, which means he means it and he is lying.
- How he talks: fast, warm, everything is "beautiful", everyone is "my friend", every promise has a date that moves.
  Swears in Greek only at machines.
- Not a stereotype: no plate smashing, no "my big fat" anything, no diner. A man who loved a room and mortgaged himself to it.
- Arc: Act 1 he hires Pedro. Act 2 he folds to Kyle and cuts Pedro loose. Act 3 he chooses, and the player's choices
  decide what he chooses (he can lend the Huracán his last speakers, or sell them to Kyle).

### 4.5 Akira Tonaka: the gym

**Akira** (Akira Tonaka). 44. Voice: pitch 0.8, rate 0.9.

- Who: Japanese-American, grew up in Sacramento, a former pro bantamweight boxer (19 fights, 14 wins, 1 broken nose that
  he says improved his face). Owns **Second Wind Boxing Club**, a boxing gym in an old body shop. Coaches amateurs, cops,
  retired bus drivers and one very determined grandmother.
- What he wants: to keep the gym. A fitness chain (FORMWORK, "strength as a service") wants the space; his landlord wants
  FORMWORK's rent. He also wants his daughter (adult, 22, in Oregon, offstage) to call more.
- His flaw: he would rather lose than ask for money. He and Pedro are the same man twenty years apart and both know it.
- To Pedro: the friend who teaches him to fight and, more importantly, when not to. His rule: "The best fight is the one you
  leave with your hands in your pockets." He becomes the older brother Pedro did not have.
- How he talks: few words, dry, loves bad country music and quotes it wrong on purpose. Never a martial-arts sage. He boxes.
  He does not bow. He eats gas-station burritos and calls them "fuel".
- Play: the gym (SIDE.md, "Gym") is his. Training raises Pedro's stamina and melee; Akira's sparring is where the player learns
  the combat keys. He is the one who stands next to Pedro at the bar fight in Act 2 and says "Pockets."

### 4.6 Clement "Clem" Ashby: the man on the corner

**Clem** (Clement Ashby). 61. Voice: pitch 0.7, rate 0.85.

- Who: born in Bed-Stuy, four years in the Navy at eighteen (he boxed on the ship, badly), then played electric bass on more records than he can count in the 1980s and 90s: session work,
  wedding bands, a disco label that paid in cash and promises. Never famous. His name is on the back of three sleeves in
  WAX DELI's bins, spelled wrong on one of them.
- How he lost the apartment: his building was "renovated" around him; a buyout he signed while his wife Dolores was dying,
  money that went to the hospital. After she died he could not stand the rooms that were left. Shelters, then a friend's
  floor, then the corner by the side wall of the old box factory that is now Mothhouse. Two years.
- His corner: he has a folding chair, a milk crate, a hand-lettered cardboard sign ("CLEM'S CORNER. WATCH YOUR CAR $0.
  TELL ME A JOKE $0."), and a small radio tuned to jazz. People who park there leave him coffee. He keeps the block's
  cars from being broken into and knows it.
- Why the fight happens (ACT1-04): Stavros's flyering crew, with Pedro, paste the Mothhouse posters over Clem's sign, then
  club security moves his chair "for the line". Clem tears the posters down and grabs Pedro's flyer bag. It is about the
  sign, which Dolores lettered for him. It is not about the flyers.
- How it is talked down: listen first (do not pick the threatening replies), give his name back ("You are Clem"), take the
  blame for the poster, and either fix the sign or put his corner on the flyer. If the player has visited WAX DELI and
  looked at the credits on a certain sleeve (a flag from ACT1-01), a reply "You played on Brooklyn Lights" ends it at once.
- If the player fights: Clem goes down, hurt. It is not funny and the camera does not linger. The flyer bag is torn,
  the line outside Mothhouse watches, and Stavros says "beautiful, now there is a video". Clem is not seen on his corner
  for two in-game days, then he is back and does not speak to Pedro until Act 2, when Pedro has to earn it.
- Arc: in Act 3 Clem plays bass on the Huracán's opening night. He is a person with a past, a skill, a sense of humour and
  his own reasons. He is never a punchline, never "crazy", never a victim to rescue. He may decline help and that is his right.

### 4.7 Kyle Strand: the man at the bar

**Kyle** (Kyle Strand). 36. Voice: pitch 0.95, rate 1.2.

- Who: from Connecticut, a former bond trader who left finance to "build community" through property. Owns a share of the
  building that holds Tincture & Ledger and is the money behind Mothhouse's sound system (Stavros's guarantee). Posts
  morning videos about cold plunges and "operating in abundance". Has a token he made for the club: MOTH, "a membership
  that appreciates".
- What he wants: to be loved by the rooms he owns. He will settle for being obeyed.
- Why the fight happens (ACT2-03): at Tincture & Ledger, Kyle humiliates the bartender (Margo, if she is Pedro's date; a
  bartender named Inés if not) over a drink, then tells Pedro his warm-up set was "great for a Tuesday" and that
  "people like you" should be grateful for the exposure. He shoves first when Pedro does not laugh.
- How it is talked down: do not touch him; make him the joke in front of his own investors (reply picker: name the token's
  price); or let Akira say "Pockets" and leave. Leaving costs Pedro the Mothhouse Friday. Fighting him costs it too, plus
  heat and a lawyer's letter. Talking him down in front of the room costs nothing but makes an enemy who is now careful.
- Not a stereotype of a class: he is a specific man, and the story gives him one human moment in Act 3 (he really did
  want a room to love him; he just tried to buy it).

### 4.8 Love interests (adults, written as people)

The player chooses who Pedro dates (ACT1-07 offers two; the others open later). A date is a short mission (SIDE.md,
"Dates"): a place, a walk, a reply picker, a mood meter. A hook-up is a choice at the end of a good date; it is an INTIMATE BEAT (section 2: suggestive, cut-away, Off = a plain fade)
and opens on a morning after. Pedro can date one person at a time "seriously"; casual hook-ups are possible and the story
notices them, gently (a text, a joke from Lana), never with punishment for its own sake. Turning someone down is always
an option and always respected.

- **Margo** (Margaret Ellison). 37. Bartender at Glenda's Long Room for eleven years. Black, Brooklyn-born, two-time
  community college dropout, reads three novels a week behind the bar, knows every regular's drink and their mother's name.
  Wants: to finish one thing she starts. Funny, guarded, allergic to men who talk about "the scene". Pedro wins her by
  asking about the book, not the bar. Voice pitch 1.0, rate 1.0.
- **Remy** (Remy Okonkwo-Hale). 33. Sound engineer at Mothhouse, Nigerian-British, moved from London for a job that turned
  out to be "audio vibes lead". Wants: a room that sounds right and a boss who knows what a limiter is. Shy until you talk
  about cables. Voice pitch 1.1, rate 1.0. (Pedro is written as open to men and women; the player chooses. The story never
  makes a thing of it.)
- **Lana** (see 4.2). Opens in Act 2 if the player has been honest with her twice. She says no once. Then "ask me after
  the Friday".
- **Adele** (see 4.3). Opens in Act 3 only, and only as a slow thing: a breakfast after Night Shift. She is too tired for
  anything else and says so; that IS the date.
- For Lana and Adele as players: Lana can date **Teodor** (Teodor Vass, 35, a pastry chef who works the night bake at a
  Lewis Avenue bakery); Adele can date **Junior** (Desmond "Junior" Pratt, 39, a dollar-van driver who has driven her home
  from four night shifts and never charged her). Outlines in ARC.md.

## 5. The places (invented names, one paragraph each, both maps)

For VENUES: a suggested real-map neighbourhood for each, by kind of street; the exact lot is VENUES' call. "Interior"
means the simple interior VENUES builds: a door, one room, the key props listed. None of these names is checked.

### 5.1 Bar one: Glenda's Long Room (the old regulars' bar)

A narrow dive on a residential avenue corner, open since 1979: a long bar, a jukebox, a pool table with one bad
pocket, wood panelling, Christmas lights all year, a photo wall of forty years of regulars. Crowd: older Black Brooklyn,
retired transit and postal workers, nurses after shift, a few young people who are welcome as long as they do not
take pictures of the photo wall. In play: Margo's bar (dates), the place to hear the neighbourhood's news and rumours
(overheard lines), the first stop of a promotion round (regulars give the best word of mouth and the smallest crowds).
Key props: bar, jukebox (plays the radio), pool table, photo wall, a back booth. Hours 14:00 to 02:00.

### 5.2 Bar two: Tincture & Ledger (the new-money cocktail bar)

A former bank branch with the vault door kept "as a feature". Edison bulbs, leather menus, cocktails with a paragraph
each, a "story" printed on the coasters about the building's history that is wrong. Crowd: new arrivals, finance and
tech, influencers filming the smoke cocktail, Kyle's investors. In play: the bar fight (ACT2-03), the best tips for a
DJ set and the worst crowd meter, where flipped clothes sell at the highest price if Lana has styled you right. The
door is strict and stupid (it reads your outfit). Key props: the vault door, a long marble bar, a DJ corner under a
taxidermy pigeon in a tiny hat. Hours 17:00 to 02:00.

### 5.3 Bar three: Sabor a Lunes (the Latin dance bar)

"Tastes like Monday": a Latin bar on a commercial avenue, red walls, a small dance floor, a back room with a cracked
mirror ball, photographs of salsa bands from the Bronx, a picture of a Colombian football team that Rafa refuses to
explain. Crowd: Puerto Rican, Dominican, Colombian, Mexican and Ecuadorian regulars, dancers of every age over 21,
couples who have danced together for thirty years and will show you. In play: Pedro's first paid gig (ACT1-02), his
Monday residency (repeatable gig), salsa and cumbia nights where the crowd meter wants older records. Key props: dance
floor, booth, mirror ball, Rafa's congas. Hours 18:00 to 03:00.

### 5.4 The after-hours: The Milk Run (venue id `afterhours`)

A plain warehouse floor behind a roll-up door on a light-industrial street, named for the early-morning milk trucks that
used to load there. Open 04:00 to 11:00: the room people go to when every other room has closed. A booth on pallets, a
coffee urn that is the only bar, folding chairs along one wall for the people who came to sit, not dance. Crowd: DJs
after their own gigs, bartenders, cooks, night workers, the people who do not want the night to end. In play: Pedro's long
"real" sets (the gigs that build his name with other DJs), Adele's Night Shift party at 8 a.m. (Act 2), the place where
the Huracán is first switched on in the yard (Act 3). Key props: booth, floor, roll-up door, coffee urn, the chairs.

Aftersloth (EXISTING, DJ Sloth's hidden room behind a door) stays as it is: in this story it is Sloth's place, the
room Pedro wants to deserve. It appears in Act 2 and Act 3 scenes with Sloth; it is not one of the seven new venues.

Venue ids (VENUES, `src/world/venues/venues.ts`): `bar-dive` = Glenda's Long Room, `bar-lounge` = Tincture & Ledger,
`bar-dance` = Sabor a Lunes (asked VENUES to rename from `bar-sports`), `afterhours` = The Milk Run, `club` = Mothhouse,
`dispensary` = Mellowdrama, `gym` = Second Wind Boxing Club. Spots: `<id>:booth`, `:bar`, `:door`, `:floor`,
`bar-dive:booth-back`, `gym:ring`, `gym:bags`, `club:sidedoor` (Clem's Corner is just outside it).

### 5.5 The club: Mothhouse

A new 900-capacity club in a former cardboard box factory on an industrial edge of the map (the north or east, near the
rail and the warehouses). Black walls, a sound system Stavros rents to it, a mezzanine VIP called "the Cocoon", lights
that go full white at the drop like a porch light (hence the name). Bash runs the door. Clem's corner is against its side
wall. In play: the warm-up slot (ACT1-08), the Friday residency Pedro is chasing, the guest list, the line that has its
own crowd meter (a long line = a good promotion round), Plush's couriers in the toilets queue (Act 2). Key props: booth,
dance floor, the Cocoon stairs, a coat check, a side door to the alley. Hours 22:00 to 04:00 Thursday to Saturday.

### 5.6 The dispensary (licensed): Mellowdrama

A licensed cannabis dispensary on a busy avenue: white shelves, a guard at the door checking ID, a "budtender" who says
