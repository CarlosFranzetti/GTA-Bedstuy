## Licence finding
VERIFIED 21 Nov 2026 from the statute itself (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1401, fetched today), not from memory:
17 U.S.C. 1401(a)(2) (Music Modernization Act, Title II) limits the federal remedy for pre-1972 sound recordings to "December 31 of the
year that is 95 years after the year of first publication" plus a transition period. (B)(i) recordings first published before 1 Jan 1923:
transition ended 31 Dec 2021. (B)(ii) recordings first published 1923 to 1946: the transition "shall end on the date that is 5 years after
the last day" of the 95-year period, so protection ends 31 Dec of (publication year + 100). A recording first published in 1925 is therefore
free on 1 Jan 2026. Today (21 Nov 2026) every US sound recording first published in 1925 or earlier is out of protection in the US; 1926
enters on 1 Jan 2027. Owner's belief confirmed. Caveats, written into the liner notes and the CREDITS entry: (1) this is US law only; (2)
it covers the SOUND RECORDING; the compositions are also free (US works published 1930 or earlier are public domain in 2026) for every
record used; (3) a date on an Internet Archive page is the uploader's, so each record below is one whose first-release year is well
documented and at most 1925, and two (1925) records are kept only because even a late-1925 release is inside the rule; (4) modern
transfers of a free recording add no new copyright in the US, and the Library of Congress items carry the Public Domain Mark.
No Mixamo-style doubt: anything whose first-release year I could not pin at or before 1925 was left out (for example Latin and Caribbean
78s: the Canaro tango on the Internet Archive has a year with no label date, so it was dropped; calypso on the Archive is 1930s or later).
