## The faces researcher reports, for the next team

**Maren (research, faces):** I can tell you why the faces read as caricature, in numbers. I set every one of our
facial traits beside real variation from two public surveys. Face width varies almost twice as much as real faces do.
How far the nose sticks out varies about two and a half times too much: that is the cartoon profile. Ears stick out
half again too much. Meanwhile every mouth is nearly the same width and every eye the same size.

**Fable:** Is it just the ranges?

**Maren:** No, and this is the bigger finding. Each trait is drawn in a way that parks about one draw in nine exactly
at its limit. With thirty traits, a typical person has three or four features stuck at the extreme. Only three people
in a hundred have none. That is the mechanism. And real features are linked: a wide face comes with a wide head.
Ours are drawn independently, so we make combinations real faces do not have.

**Maren:** On the eyes: keep the new lids and put a real eyeball behind them with a slight bulge. A flat painted iris
has no depth, so at an angle it looks pasted on. I think that is the "wonky". Also one eye can sit up to 2.4 mm
higher than the other on a 10 mm opening: far too much.
