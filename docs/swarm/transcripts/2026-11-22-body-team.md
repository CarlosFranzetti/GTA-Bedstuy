## The faces researcher reports, for the next team

**Maren (research, faces):** I can tell you why the faces read as caricature, in numbers. I set every one of our
facial traits beside real variation from two public surveys. Face width varies almost twice as much as real faces do.
How far the nose sticks out varies about two and a half times too much: that is the cartoon profile. Ears stick out
half again too much. Meanwhile every mouth is nearly the same width and every eye the same size.

**Fable:** Is it just the ranges?

**Maren:** No, and this is the bigger finding. Each trait is drawn in a way that parks about one draw in nine exactly
at its limit. With thirty traits, a typical person has three or four features stuck at the extreme. Only three people
in a hundred have none. That is the mechanism. And real features are linked: a wide face comes with a wide head.
Ours are drawn independently, so we make combinations real faces do not have.

**Maren:** On the eyes: keep the new lids and put a real eyeball behind them with a slight bulge. A flat painted iris
has no depth, so at an angle it looks pasted on. I think that is the "wonky". Also one eye can sit up to 2.4 mm
higher than the other on a 10 mm opening: far too much.

**Fable:** That is the faces team's first week, already written. It waits until the body team is done.

## The first hour: three causes found

**Nadia (Clothes):** Found the dark patches, and they are not what any of us thought. It is not torn geometry. It is
the texture. When a person's skin and clothes are painted, the leg pieces are added to the list after a correction that
only covers the earlier pieces. So every leg vertex gets a position of "not a number". In the painting step a broken
corner like that wins every texel it touches. That is the black.

**Fable:** That may also explain a warning I chased this afternoon and could not catch: one boot in five logged a
geometry with broken numbers in it. Same family of bug. Good.

**Nadia:** Shoes next. They were a fixed 25 centimetres in front of the ankle whatever the person's size, with a
floor on how small they could get. So a man had 31 to 35 centimetre shoes and a child had clown shoes. They are now
0.158 of height, against a real foot of 0.154. The shoe-hem check that has been red for days is green.

**Corin (Body):** Arms first. The elbow and wrist sat too high, the upper arm was a fifth short and the hand a third
short, which is why hands hung at the hip. I re-spaced the arm once, when a body loads, so all three detail levels and
the skeleton move together. Fingertips now land at 0.374 of height. Real is 0.375. Heads were four percent big on
adults; they are 7.5 heads tall now. Shoulders are eight percent wider.

**Corin:** Nadia, two tests of yours went red after your shoe change: one elder's shoe is now shorter than twice its
width. And I may have nudged your hem check with my belly change. Telling you rather than touching it.
