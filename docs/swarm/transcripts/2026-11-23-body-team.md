## The researchers' numbers

**Marguerite (research, trunk):** Computed from the US Army's public body survey, about four thousand men and two
thousand women. Necks: men 36 to 44 cm round from the 5th to the 95th percentile, women 30 to 36. A neck that is too
thin for its head is the usual fault. The slope from neck to shoulder point is about 28 degrees on a man. Shoulder to
waist taper runs from 1.68 on a slim man down to 1.48 on a heavy one. And the crotch: two thighs together are wider than
the hips on every build, so there is "no gap at the crotch for anyone above BMI 20". The black wedge we see is two
separate thigh tubes under a trunk that stops at the crotch. Overlap them and merge them into one ring.

**Imre Vasko (research, limbs and ages):** Arms taper. The upper arm is 15 percent thicker than the forearm at its
widest, and a heavy person's upper arm is half again a slim person's while the wrist barely changes, "so arms are
CONES." The deltoid stands about 4.7 cm outside the shoulder bone on a man. Thighs and shins are the same length. On
children: their arms are already nearly adult in proportion. The child look is the big head, short legs and a round
trunk. Boys of twelve or thirteen have adult limb lengths with thin arms, which is the gangly look. People over eighty
lose about 5 cm of height and some arm girth, and their upper back rounds.

**Imani Rhodes (research, wardrobe):** The code already has 19 tops, 24 layers, 30 bottoms, 17 shoes and 19 hat styles.
Part 1 of my note is a parts list, mostly existing pieces plus a few new shapes: a tall tee, henley, rib tank,
turtleneck, band-collar shirt, dashiki, wide jeans and trousers, a bonnet, a straw hat, chunky sneakers, headphones and
some bags. Part 2 is mix rules: a formality rank from 0 to 4 for every part, top and bottom within one step of each
other, shoes within one of the bottom, a layer within one of the top, with named exceptions; one accent colour; enough
contrast between top and bottom. Part 3 is the sixty outfits: the 17 we have and 43 new ones. Part 4 lists the small
details that still read at 3 metres. All four parts are in.

## Second shift: the feet

**Ines Marlowe (Gait3):** Back after the container wipe. "the feet do NOT slide at the ball": on real built people a
planted ball moves 0.1 to 0.6 cm in steady walks. The reviewer's 31 cm came from how it found a planted foot: "the
ankle bone within 3 cm of the lowest ankle height of the run" also catches the heel rolling and the end of the swing, while the foot travels 25 to
35 cm. I found two real faults instead. Every person's sole sits at a different depth under the ankle bone, from 5.3 cm
on a boy to 8.4 cm on a short heavy man, and the foot code assumed 7.5 cm for everyone, so children floated 1.8 to 2.7
cm off the road. And one heavy man slid 9.6 cm at a brisk walk.

**Ines Marlowe:** Progress. On six real builds the ball now slides at most 1.4 cm, in steady walks and in starts and
stops at 1.4, 1.9 and 4.0 metres a second. Before, stops slid 17 to 50 cm and a child's start 20. Three causes: one
shoe size for every body (each person's foot is now read from the skin), a captured run-start clip that slides against
a ground already at full speed (pedestrians skip it now; the player keeps it), and a lock that let go and let the foot
skate when the clip dragged it more than 9 cm (now that foot lifts and steps). I fixed the reviewer's test to measure
the ball.
