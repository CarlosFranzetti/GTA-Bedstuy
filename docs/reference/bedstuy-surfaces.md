# Bed-Stuy surface reference (for texture work)

Purpose: a text-only brief of what real surfaces look like, so texture artists can paint procedural equivalents. No images were downloaded or copied. Hex values are **estimates** unless a source is cited; calibrate them by eye against photos you have a right to use.

**Access limits (read first):** During research, the session egress proxy refused connections to `en.wikipedia.org` and `www.nyc.gov` (403 policy denial), and WebFetch could not resolve `www.brownstoner.com`. The LPC designation report and the NYC DOT Street Design Manual were therefore **not read**. Facts below come from search-result snippets and secondary sources, and are marked where thin. Verify against the primary documents before treating any number as final.

Status legend: **[S]** supported by a cited source (snippet level); **[U]** unverified or general knowledge; **[X]** conflicting sources.

## 1. Brownstone and other facades

- **Material:** Portland brownstone is a Portland Formation sandstone with feldspar and hematite; freshly cut it is pink and weathers to brown ([NJ Geological Survey](https://www.nj.gov/dep/njgs/enviroed/infocirc/brownstone.pdf) [S]; [Brownstoner Q&A](https://www.brownstoner.com/brooklyn-life/charles-lockwoo) [S]). Shades range from pinkish brown to dark chocolate; some stone reads red-orange or purplish ([Brick Underground](https://www.brickunderground.com/live/facts-about-brownstones) [S]).
- **Veneer:** Most brownstones are brick buildings with a thin stone facade ([Brick Underground](https://www.brickunderground.com/live/facts-about-brownstones) [S]). Paint the stone and brick as separate layers.
- **Source quarries:** Portland, Connecticut quarries; the site closed in 2012 ([Elika](https://www.elikarealestate.com/blog/?p=54828) [S]).
- **Finish:** "Rough stone" facades appear on Romanesque Revival examples in Bed-Stuy (Bainbridge St) ([Brownstoner listing snippets] [S]). Rock-faced vs tooled vs smooth terminology is **not sourced** [U].
- **Course heights:** not sourced [U]. Use horizontal joints at regular intervals; keep them subtle.
- **Soot/weathering:** darker lower floors and window heads are typical urban soiling [U]. No source found.
- **Painted brownstone and painted brick:** no source on Bed-Stuy paint colours found [U]. Painted brick in the area is common, but palettes are unsourced.
- **Romanesque/Renaissance details:** metal cornice with a curved projecting bay (297 Hancock St, c.1892) [S]; arched windows and door hood on rough stone (Bainbridge St) [S]; terra cotta reliefs on a Renaissance Revival front (Macon St) [S]. Italianate and Romanesque Revival houses are both present [S].
- **Designation context:** Expanded Stuyvesant Heights Historic District, designated 16 April 2013, about 825 buildings ([LPC report LP-2496](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/2496.pdf) [S, not read]; [CityLand](https://www.citylandnyc.org/bed-stuyexpanded-stuyvesant-heights-historic-district-designated-capping-decades-long-process/) [S]). Bedford Historic District lies between Monroe and Fulton streets and Bedford and Tompkins avenues [S].

**What to paint:** Mid-to-dark brown stone base with faint pink undertone, soot darkening toward the bottom and below sills, brick walls in muted red-brown with pale mortar joints.

## 2. Stoops, cornices, lintels, fire escapes

- **Stoop treads:** brownstone; replacements are cast to match existing stone ([Brownstoner forum/renovation](https://www.brownstoner.com/forum-archive/2010/03/love-iron-rails) [S]).
- **Cast-iron railings and newel posts:** 1880s floral-pattern newel posts were common on Brooklyn stoops ([seller description, unverified] [S, weak]); replica posts are cast from neighbourhood originals ([Brownstoner newel post notes](https://www.brownstoner.com/interiors-renovation/newel-post-notions/) [S]). Railing parts resemble 1900–1920 Brooklyn patterns ([Brownstoner forum](https://www.brownstoner.com/forum-archive/2010/03/love-iron-rails) [S]). Masonry newel posts are an approved alternative [S].
- **Cast-iron colour:** typically black or dark green paint [U].
- **Cornices:** pressed metal and bracketed cornices are common [U]; the metal cornice on 297 Hancock St is confirmed [S]. Painted colours not sourced.
- **Window lintels and sills:** stone, often a contrasting tone to the facade [U].
- **Fire escapes:** black painted iron [U]. No source found.

**What to paint:** Black or dark-green iron with thin highlights on rail tops. Stone stoop treads with worn edges.

## 3. Street furniture and sidewalks

- **Sidewalk materials:** DOT's manual lists unpigmented concrete, tinted concrete, bluestone flag and granite slab ([NYC DOT SDM](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/sdm_lores.pdf) [S]). Standard concrete uses a simulated saw-cut joint pattern of 5 × 5 scoring, and historic districts may use concrete tinted to look like bluestone or granite ([Public Design Commission](https://www.nyc.gov/site/designcommission/design-review/design-guidelines/distinctive-sidewalks.page) [S]). Slab size: 5 ft squares implied by the scoring; not stated as a slab dimension [U].
- **Bluestone colour:** grey-blue; hex estimate only. Survival in the neighbourhood not verified [U].
- **Tree pits:** NYC Parks' 2008 standards give 4 × 10 ft or 5 × 10 ft as the optimum, and sidewalk width often limits this ([NYC Parks standards](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/trees/standards.pdf) [S, dated; check current]). Tree guards are typically about 10 ft long, 5 ft max wide, 18 in min height, three-sided, at least 12 in from the curb ([NYC Street Design](https://www.nycstreetdesign.info/furniture/tree-guards) [S]). A Parks tree-work permit is required.
- **Curbs:** granite curb colour and height not verified [U]; use grey granite.
- **Hydrants:** NFPA 291 cap colours: red under 500 gpm, orange 500–999, green 1,000–1,499, blue above 1,500 ([QRFS](https://blog.qrfs.com/286-fire-hydrant-colors-their-nfpa-spectrum-and-meaning/) [S, general US]). The NYC scheme is **not confirmed** [X]: one source says NYC hydrants are often black or silver, another says chrome yellow with coloured caps.
- **Streetlights:** LED cobra head on a tapered steel octagonal pole is the standard; luminaires are 78 W residential, 110 W wider streets ([NYC DOT Lighting SDM 2020](https://www.nycstreetdesign.info/sites/default/files/2020-03/Lighting_SDM_v3_2020_0.pdf) [S]). Pole finish: silver, black or green in 2020; older text said black, brown or green [X]. Mounting height not confirmed [U]; the davit alternative is 30 ft [S].
- **Street signs:** green, white reflective lettering, all-caps text; green is the citywide standard ([PIX11](https://digital-release.pix11.com/news/local-news/exploring-nycs-colorful-street-signs-green-brown-blue-black-and-honorary/amp/) [S]; [Forgotten NY](https://forgotten-ny.com/2011/09/changing-codes/amp) [S]).
- **Litter baskets:** older green wire-mesh baskets are being replaced by plastic Better Bins (basket, lid, stand) from fall 2023 ([DSNY](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/collection/containerization/litter-basket-of-the-future.page) [S]). Expect mixed old and new bins.
- **Bike racks, mailboxes, bus stops, parking signs:** not sourced [U].
- **Curb cuts and manhole covers:** not sourced [U].

**What to paint:** Concrete with a 5 × 5 ft joint grid and some staining; occasional bluestone in older blocks. Tree pits as dark mulch rings inside black or grey guards. Green street-name signs. Dark-grey cobra poles.

## 4. Roadway

- **Asphalt:** not sourced [U]. Use dark grey-black when new and lighter grey with patches when old.
- **Crosswalks:** ladder style is common in the city, but NYC's stripe dimensions were not found [U]. Generic US ladder bars are 12–24 in wide and 12–24 in apart ([Wikipedia snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crosswalks_in_North_America) [S, generic]).
- **Lane lines:** double yellow centreline and white edge lines are standard practice [U].
- **Manholes:** round iron covers with raised lettering are typical [U].
- **Parking:** kerb-side parking with metal regulation signs [U].

**What to paint:** Asphalt in dark grey with light patches. White ladder bars at intersections. Yellow centre lines.

## 5. Storefronts on Throop, Marcus Garvey and Tompkins

- **Bodega awnings:** corrugated metal awnings in yellow and red with bold graphics are the defining look ([Leifman, Brooklyn Bodega](https://americanethnologist.org/products/brooklyn-bodega/231822445/) [S, book description]). Specific hex values not sourced.
- **Roll-down gates:** open-grille gates are common; awnings add depth to storefronts ([NYC SBS Storefront Guide](https://home4.nyc.gov/assets/sbs/downloads/pdf/neighborhoods/storefront-guide.pdf) [S]). Galvanised corrugated gates are common [U].
- **Signs:** hand-painted and lightbox signs; the Bed-Stuy storefront signage record is a book, not a web source ([Signs & Artifacts No. 4](https://shop.icp.org/signs-artifacts-no-4-bed-stuy.html) [S]). Colours not sourced.
- **Laundromats, barbershops, storefront churches:** no source found [U].
- **New condos:** no sources on current Throop/Marcus Garvey new builds; 639 Gates Ave (seven storeys, 112 units) is a nearby example ([search snippet] [S]).

**What to paint:** Corner stores with red-and-yellow corrugated awnings, dark galvanised roll gates with graffiti tags, plain painted signboards.

## 6. Roofs

- **Water tanks:** wooden rooftop tanks are standard on taller buildings; makers include Rosenwach, Isseks Brothers and American Pipe & Tank ([6sqft](https://www.6sqft.com/nyc-water-towers-history-use-and-infrastructure/) [S]). Tanks hold about 5,000–10,000 gal and last 30–35 years [S]. Counts conflict: 17,000 (6sqft) vs 5,600 (Vital City) [X]. Source: [Vital City](https://www.vitalcitynyc.org/nyc-wooden-water-tanks-architecture-history/).
- **Roof surfaces:** tar and gravel or membrane, typically dark [U]. No source found.
- **Parapets, bulkheads, satellite dishes:** not sourced [U].

**What to paint:** Weathered cedar-brown tanks on steel legs; dark roofs with gravel specks; parapet caps in stone or brick.
