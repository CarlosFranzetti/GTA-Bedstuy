# What people on a Brooklyn street wear (for the CLOTHES seat)

Author: Imani Rhodes (RESEARCH, clothes), 22 Nov 2026. Status: parts 1 to 4 written; texture assets in part 3 were checked
against the Poly Haven and ambientCG public APIs and licence pages on 22 Nov (details in part 3).

How to read this. The outfits and the cloth checklist are my own synthesis of widely documented street dress and of
standard garment-pattern practice. They are NOT quoted from one source, and I did not open per-outfit photo sources
(no photo is named as a reference below, so none needs a licence). Treat numbers as starting values to tune by
looking. Where I say "ease" I mean garment minus body, in cm, measured round the chest or waist. No lettering, logos
or numbers on any garment (MEMORY.md rule 4). Palette rule from DIRECTION.md: black, navy, grey, olive, brown, denim,
off-white, at most one accent per person; albedo about 0.04 to 0.75; a white shirt about 0.72.

Style reference only: GTA V shows how specific and layered street clothing can be (fitted, worn, mismatched, seasonal).
Nothing is taken from it.

Ease key: SKIN-CLOSE 0 to 4 cm; REGULAR 8 to 12; RELAXED 14 to 20; OVERSIZED 24 to 36 (hem often at mid-thigh, shoulder
seam 4 to 8 cm past the shoulder point). Shoulder seam: NATURAL (on the bone), DROPPED (3 to 8 cm past it).
Hem anchors: HIP (iliac crest + about 15 cm, roughly crotch-line minus 8), MID-THIGH, KNEE.

## 1. Twelve to sixteen whole outfits

Respect note. Bed-Stuy is historically Black, with Caribbean (Jamaican, Trinidadian, Haitian), West African, Latino and
newer residents, and Hasidic neighbours to the north. These looks are everyday dress, chosen as ordinary clothes people
wear to church, work, school and the corner shop. Build them as clothes, not as costume: no exaggerated patterns, no
"ethnic" prints as a shortcut. Keep cultural pieces (head wraps, kufis, church hats, durags, modest long skirts) as
plain forms in plain colours, on varied ages and body types, never on every person of a given skin tone.

Fabric behaviour words used: STIFF (holds its own shape: canvas, heavy denim, starched cotton), LIMP (jersey: hangs,
clings, wrinkles at joints), PUFFY (quilted nylon: rounded baffles, soft silhouette, creases between baffles),
HEAVY (wool coating, leather: falls straight, thick hems, little ripple).

### O1. Church Sunday, older woman (60 to 80, styles from the 1980s to 2000s; any season, spring to fall)
- Hat: wide-brim church hat as a shape: crown 8 to 10 cm high, brim 8 to 12 cm, slightly tilted; felt or straw; navy,
  cream, plum-brown, grey. Hat is the one accent allowed (a hat band in a different tone) or the coat.
- Dress or skirt suit: jacket REGULAR (ease chest 10, waist 8), NATURAL shoulder with a slight pad (+1 cm), hip-length
  hem, 3-quarter or wrist sleeve, nearly straight (cuff only 3 cm narrower than the upper arm),
  notch lapel, 2 to 3 buttons. Skirt: straight, hem at mid-calf (just below knee by 8 to 12 cm).
- Fabric: crepe or wool blend: HEAVY-ish, matte, slightly creased at elbow and seat. Colours: navy, charcoal, plum-brown,
  cream blouse (0.65 albedo, not white). Wear: shine on seat and elbows, faint pilling on cuffs.
- Shoes: low block-heel court shoe, 4 to 5 cm heel, or a flat loafer. Bag: structured handbag on the forearm (rect, 24 x
  18 cm). Optional: glasses on a chain, a plain brooch (dot).

### O2. Church Sunday, older man (55 to 80, 1990s to 2000s cut)
- Suit: jacket REGULAR to RELAXED (ease chest 12 to 16), shoulder NATURAL with pad, hem at mid-crotch (covers the seat),
  sleeve to the wrist bone with 1 cm of shirt cuff showing, notch lapel, two-button. Trousers: high-ish rise (navel minus
  3 cm), straight leg, opening 22 to 24 cm round, full break (the hem rests on the shoe and folds once).
- Shirt: pressed cotton, point collar, collar stand 3 cm, tie at the neck (plain, no pattern: dark, one-tone).
- Hat (optional): brimmed trilby or fedora shape: brim 5 to 6 cm, crown 11 cm with a pinch; felt, dark grey or brown.
- Fabric: wool suiting STIFF-HEAVY with a faint sheen at knees and seat; crisp cotton shirt with a vertical crease.
- Colours: charcoal, navy, brown; shirt off-white (0.70) or pale blue (desaturated); one accent: a burgundy tie or a
  mustard pocket square. Shoes: leather dress shoe, cap or plain toe, black or oxblood, sole 1 cm, heel 3 cm.

### O3. Older man, errand-day: kufi or cap, long shirt (50 to 75; spring to fall)
- Kufi as a shape: a round close-fitting cap, 5 to 8 cm high, with a fine knit or woven texture. Colours: white,
  cream, black, brown, dark green. OR a knit kangol-style flat cap as a shape: low round crown with a short peak, a few
  cm of slouch over the front; wool or cotton; grey, navy, olive.
- Top: long shirt or tunic: hem at mid-thigh to knee, REGULAR to RELAXED (chest ease 14 to 18), mandarin (band) collar
  stand 2.5 cm with a short placket, long sleeve, cotton; solid colour (white, cream, sand, olive).
- Trousers: matching or contrasting straight trousers, rise at the navel, leg 22 cm round at ankle, hem touching the
  shoe top. Sandal or leather loafer. Wear: a few pressed-in creases at the elbow and lap, hem darkened by street dirt.

### O4. Older woman, daily wear, Caribbean or West African heritage: head wrap and long skirt (45 to 75)
- Head wrap: a soft cap of cloth wound round the head: crown height 6 to 10 cm above the hairline, a knot or tuck at
  front or side that adds 3 to 4 cm, 2 to 3 folds visible; one solid colour (not a pattern shortcut): ochre, deep green,
  rust, navy, off-white; a soft cotton or a crisp starched one.
- Top: blouse or loose tee, RELAXED (chest ease 16 to 20), hem at hip, three-quarter sleeve, round or V collar.
  Skirt: ankle length or mid-calf, A-line, waist ease 8 to 10 at an elasticated or gathered waist, fall straight; cotton
  limp-to-medium. Colour: plain navy, black, olive, with a pale blouse. A cardigan for cool days: hip hem, relaxed.
- Shoes: sturdy sandal or low flat. Bag: a shopping bag or a small cart (see accessories). Glasses optional.

### O5. Older man: 2000s tall tee, baggy denim, tracksuit mix (40 to 60, from the 2000s; any season)
- Tall tee: OVERSIZED, chest ease 30 to 36, hem at upper-thigh, shoulder DROPPED 5 to 7 cm, short sleeve to mid-forearm
  (sleeve elbow-length is the tell), round collar rib 2 cm. Cotton jersey, slightly LIMP-heavy (200 gsm): it hangs,
  ripples at the hem. White tee is off-white 0.70 only; or grey, black, navy.
- Baggy jeans: low-to-mid rise, straight and wide leg: thigh ease 20 to 26, knee 26 cm round width... ankle opening 24
  to 28 cm, hem at the heel with stacking of 1 to 2 folds over the shoe and the back dragging below the heel.
  Heavy stiff denim: broad vertical creases from hip to knee, whisker fade at the lap, darker stripe where the leg rubs.
- Alternative: tracksuit (jacket + trouser, matching, nylon shell, cuffs ribbed): jacket REGULAR to RELAXED hem at hip
  with a ribbed band 6 cm, trouser straight with an elastic cuff or a straight open hem; colours navy, black, forest
  green, burgundy, with ONE contrast stripe at the side seam (a plain 2 cm stripe, no lettering).
- Shoes: retro basketball high-top (white, grey) or work boot. Accessory: flat-brim cap as a shape, or kangol-style cap.
  Chain: a simple rope or link, 3 mm, tucked or hanging 20 cm.

### O6. Older man: leather jacket and slacks, a 1990s look (50 to 75; autumn to winter)
- Jacket: leather bomber or short moto: hip-length hem, REGULAR (chest ease 12), shoulder natural, sleeve to the wrist
  with a ribbed or zipped cuff, standing collar 4 cm or a flat collar; zip front. Leather: HEAVY, a thick folded hem,
  few ripples but 3 to 4 deep creases at the elbow and across the stomach; worn lighter at shoulders, cuffs, pockets,
  elbows. Black or dark brown.
- Under: knit sweater or collared shirt, off-white or grey. Trousers: slacks, straight, rise at the navel, ankle opening
  23 cm, slight break; charcoal. Shoes: leather loafer or dress shoe.
- Hat: a wool flat cap, or kangol-style cap as a shape.

### O7. Middle-aged worker, trades: canvas work jacket and carpenter trousers (30 to 55; autumn to winter)
- Jacket: canvas duck "chore" or "work" jacket: hip hem, REGULAR to RELAXED (chest ease 14), shoulder natural, sleeve to
  the wrist, 3 cm cuff, flat collar with a stand of 2 cm (corduroy collar optional), button or zip front; 2 chest and 2
  hip patch pockets (patch shapes, no logo). Fabric STIFF: keeps a boxy shape, creases sharply at the elbow, folded
  hem; colours tan, brown, olive, black (faded, lighter at shoulders, elbows, cuffs and the back of the forearm).
- Under: thermal knit or a hooded sweatshirt, grey or navy; grey beanie as a shape (rolled cuff 5 cm).
- Trousers: carpenter or work pants: rise at the navel minus 2, straight leg, ankle opening 24 cm; hem breaks once over
  the boot; a tool loop and a hammer pocket as shapes; canvas STIFF; knees stained darker; hems frayed and pale.
- Boots: work boot, ankle height 15 to 18 cm, sole 3 cm with a lugged edge, tan or brown leather, scuffed toe.

### O8. Middle-aged worker, city and utility: hi-vis vest, hard shape cap, cargo trousers (30 to 55; spring to fall)
- Hi-vis vest worn over a grey or navy hooded sweatshirt or work shirt: vest hip length, RELAXED (chest ease 20, open
  at the sides or zip front), two horizontal reflective bands (silver-grey, 5 cm) and two vertical across the shoulders
  as plain bands; the vest is the ONE accent (fluorescent yellow-green desaturated to about 70 percent saturation so
  it fits the muted world, orange for road crews). Fabric: thin polyester mesh or knit, LIMP, slightly translucent; the
  band catches light.
- Trousers: cargo or work trousers, straight, ankle opening 24 cm, with thigh pocket shapes; navy or charcoal; dusty
  at knees and hem. Boots: work boot with a steel toe shape (rounder, 3 cm higher at the toe).
- Hard hat or plain cap as a shape. No insignia, no lettering.

### O9. Middle-aged worker, healthcare: scrubs and fleece (25 to 55, any season; shifts at all hours)
- Scrub top: V-neck, short sleeve, hem at hip with side slits, REGULAR to RELAXED (chest ease 14), shoulder natural;
  scrub trouser: straight with a drawcord waist (rise navel minus 2), ankle opening 22 cm, hem at the shoe top. Cotton-
  poly: LIMP, wrinkles at the elbow, thigh and crotch, a soft crease down the shin. Colours: navy, ceil-blue (muted),
  teal, grey, maroon. No insignia, no name tag.
- Over it for the street: a zip fleece or a puffer vest: fleece hip length, RELAXED, a stand collar 4 cm, textured and
  fuzzy (thickness 8 to 12 mm), grey or navy; lanyard as a thin line and a plain badge shape (blank). Backpack or tote.
- Shoes: clog-like or clean white running shoe (see shoes). Colour: nothing white-white; off-white 0.70.

### O10. Middle-aged, commute: uniform shirt and trouser, no insignia (transit, security, delivery; 25 to 55)
- Shirt: short or long sleeve collared uniform shirt, REGULAR (chest ease 12), shoulder natural, hem tucked or at the
  hip; collar stand 3 cm with two chest pocket shapes; poly-cotton: STIFF-ish and slightly shiny at the shoulders; navy,
  grey, light blue. Trousers: pressed straight, crease down the front, ankle opening 22 cm, belt as a 3.5 cm band.
- Over it: a zip jacket in navy or black, hip hem. Shoes: black work shoe or boot; high-top in black. No patches.
- Delivery variant: black shell jacket, cross-body bag, reflective bands (plain) on the backpack, a helmet as a shape.

### O11. Young adult, winter street: puffer, wide trousers, clean sneakers (18 to 35; December to February; today)
- Puffer: a cropped or hip-length puffer: hem at the waist (cropped, +10 cm above hip) or at mid-thigh, OVERSIZED to
  RELAXED (chest ease 24 to 32; looks big because baffles add 30 to 40 mm of loft), baffles 12 to 18 cm tall, hood or
  high collar 8 cm stand, front zip over a flap, elasticated or ribbed cuff; PUFFY nylon with a faint sheen and
  creases in the pinches between baffles, collapsed at the elbow and under the arms; colours black, navy, olive, stone,
  or one accent (a deep red, a mustard).
- Trousers: wide-leg or straight cargo, rise at the navel, thigh ease 24, ankle opening 30 to 34 cm, hem stacking on the
  shoe by 2 to 4 cm; denim or twill: STIFF-medium, in dark wash or black; hem darkened by street slush.
- Under: hooded sweatshirt (see O13), knit beanie, scarf wound twice, tote bag. Shoes: clean sneaker (white leather
  with a cream sole, grey suede), sole 2.5 to 3.5 cm. Gloves: plain black.

### O12. Young adult, mid-season: oversized coat and slim joggers, cropped jacket (18 to 35; spring and autumn; today)
- Oversized coat: a wool coating overcoat: hem at the knee, OVERSIZED (chest ease 28), shoulder DROPPED 6 cm, notch
  lapel 9 cm wide, single breasted with a covered placket, sleeve 3 cm longer than the wrist so the cuff reaches the
  base of the thumb. HEAVY wool: smooth, long vertical folds, a thick hem band (12 mm), a sharp edge at the collar
  roll. Colours: camel (desaturated), charcoal, black, olive.
- Or cropped jacket: a short bomber or a work jacket cropped to the waist, RELAXED (chest ease 14), canvas or nylon.
- Trousers: slim jogger: skin-close at the calf, tapering from thigh ease 10 to an ankle opening 14 cm with a ribbed
  cuff that rests above the shoe; heavy jersey or fleece: soft, ripples at the knee. Or wide trousers (see O11).
- Under: a plain tee, SKIN-CLOSE to REGULAR. Shoes: clean sneaker. Bag: tote or sling on the chest. Glasses or a cap.

### O13. Young adult, summer: shorts, tee and slides, or sundress (18 to 35; June to August; today)
- Tee: boxy, RELAXED to OVERSIZED (chest ease 18 to 30), cropped at the hip or long at the thigh, shoulder DROPPED 4 cm,
  sleeve to mid-upper-arm, crew collar rib 2.2 cm; cotton jersey, heavy 220 gsm: holds a boxy shape and does not cling.
- Shorts: knee-length (hem 3 cm above the knee), RELAXED, ease 20 at the thigh, leg opening 30 cm; cotton twill or nylon,
  stiff; or cut-off denim with a frayed edge. Or a long cotton sundress: straight, limp, hem at mid-calf.
- Shoes: slides (thick strap, sole 3 cm) or a clean low sneaker, white or neutral socks. Bag: cross-body, 22 x 16 cm.
- Accessories: sunglasses, wired headphones around the neck, a hoop earring.

### O14. Teenager: school day (13 to 18; today; autumn to spring)
- Hoodie: RELAXED to OVERSIZED (chest ease 20 to 30), hem at the hip, ribbed hem band 6 cm, kangaroo pocket, drawcord
  hood folded to a 12 cm rolled collar; heavy cotton fleece: soft, wide, ripples at the elbow, a hem that rolls. Colours:
  grey, black, navy, olive, off-white, with the one accent as a bag, a cap or a sneaker.
- Trousers: cargo or baggy jeans (wide-leg), or slim joggers; hem stacked on the shoe. School uniform variant: navy
  polo or cardigan, grey trouser or pleated skirt (hem 5 cm above the knee), plain; no insignia.
- Shoes: sneakers worn at the toe. Backpack: 40 cm high, both straps, slung over one shoulder half the time.
- Children (5 to 12): proportionally larger head and shorter torso (BODY seat sets this); trouser opening 18 to 20 cm,
  hooded jacket with ease 14, bright but muted colours ok (one accent, e.g. a yellow raincoat); sneakers with
  velcro; backpack at 28 cm high; hat or hood. Never lettering or characters on clothes.

### O15. Hasidic neighbour (North, the Williamsburg edge): modest dress, walking through (20 to 70; all seasons)
- Respectful, plain, no caricature. Men: dark long coat (a frock-style coat to the knee or mid-calf, black wool,
  REGULAR ease 12, plain), white shirt, black trousers, black brimmed hat (brim 4 to 6 cm, crown 11 cm) or a dark
  velvet skullcap, shoes black leather. Women: modest long skirt (below the knee to mid-calf), long-sleeved top with a
  high neckline, tights, a headcovering (a scarf or a wig, not shown as a costume): colours black, navy, grey, brown.
- Include only where the map puts them (the northern edge), a few figures, varied ages, ordinary behaviour. Do not
  make these a joke or a mascot; no ringlets or beards as a gag shape. If the seat is unsure, keep it simple and ask
  the owner (the lead decides) before adding.

### O16. Young adult, working a street job: delivery cyclist or vendor (20 to 40; all seasons)
- Shell jacket, black or navy nylon, hip length, RELAXED, hood, zip, a reflective strip; stretch trousers (slim jogger
  or work pant); cap or beanie; cross-body delivery bag as a rectangle (35 x 35 cm); sneakers with wear at the toe.
- Vendor variant: apron (canvas or cotton), long sleeves rolled to the elbow, rolled cap, work shoes with a thicker
  sole; plain, no logos.

## 2. Shoes and accessories at 3 m

At 3 m only the silhouette, the sole thickness and the height at the ankle show. Sole = outsole plus midsole stack at the
heel. Ankle height = from the ground to the top edge of the shoe at the ankle bone. Measurements are adult sizes.

| Shoe | Silhouette (side view) | Sole at heel | Height at ankle | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Running sneaker | long, low toe spring, rounded, a heel that flares slightly | 3 to 4 cm, thicker than the toe by 1.5 cm | 7 to 8 cm (ends below the ankle bone) | mesh upper looks matte; the sole is a pale band; wear is at the toe crease |
| Retro basketball high-top | bulky, padded tongue, a round toe box, a flat cupsole | 3 cm, a flat clean band | 14 to 16 cm (covers the ankle bone) | white and grey are more common than colour; creases at the toe box; leather shines |
| Clean low sneaker (court style) | slim, rounded toe, a flat thin sole | 2 to 2.5 cm | 8 cm | white leather with a cream sole; keep it off pure white |
| Work boot | square-ish toe, stout, a defined heel (2.5 cm) | 3 to 4 cm, lugged | 15 to 20 cm, lace to the top | scuffed toe, dark at the welt, trouser hem breaks over it |
| Loafer / dress shoe | slim, a pointed or rounded toe, a heel | 1 to 1.5 cm sole, heel 2.5 to 3 cm | 7 cm | leather shine on the toe; creases across the vamp |
| Slide | a flat open sole with a wide strap over the instep | 2.5 to 3.5 cm, foam | 3 cm | bare foot and ankle show; socks sometimes |
| Winter boot | rounded, insulated, thick | 4 to 5 cm | 20 to 28 cm | trouser hem tucked in or stacked over; salt marks at the base |
| Court shoe (heel) | pointed or round toe, a 4 to 6 cm heel | 1 cm sole | 8 cm at the instep | for church and office wear |

Bags (simple forms): cross-body (rect 22 x 16 x 6 cm, strap across the chest and a diagonal line over the torso); tote
(rect 38 x 40 cm, double handles hanging 20 cm; canvas, limp; carried on the shoulder or forearm); backpack (35 x 45 x 15
cm, rounded top, 2 straps; slung on one shoulder by teenagers); shopping cart (a 2-wheel wire frame 40 x 90 cm, pulled
behind, with a bag on it; older people); sling (a small pack 20 x 12 x 8 cm across the chest); a plastic grocery bag
(a bulb 30 cm tall, swinging at the knee).

Hats: kangol-style flat cap (a low round crown, a short peak, wool); baseball cap (6 cm brim; do NOT add a logo); beanie
(rolled cuff 5 cm, 20 cm tall, snug); wide church hat (above); kufi (above); bucket hat (a 4 cm drooping brim); durag
(a smooth tight cap on the skull with a tail of 20 cm at the back; satin sheen; varied wearers, plain colours); head
wrap (above); hood (up or down; a 12 cm rolled collar when down).

Scarves: a wool scarf wound twice, 12 cm thick at the neck, the tail falling to the chest; a thin scarf in a knot.
Glasses: a rectangular or round frame, 1.5 cm band, thin or thick; sunglasses are darker, 4.5 cm lens. Headphones: on-
ear (a band over the head, 5 cm cups) or earbuds (a thin cable, tiny); around the neck at times. Jewellery as simple
forms: a thin chain (1.5 to 3 mm) at the collar, a hoop earring (diameter 2 to 4 cm), a ring, a watch (a 3 cm dial on a
strap), a bangle. No lettering, no logo, no pendants with a text.

- Per-asset facts below come from the sites' own public APIs (https://api.polyhaven.com/assets, /files/<id>, and
  https://ambientcg.com/api/v2/full_json), fetched on 22 Nov; ambientCG asset page https://ambientcg.com/a/Fabric048 was

- "Tiles" below: Poly Haven markets these as seamless PBR textures, but I did not test seams; ambientCG pages carry a
  tiling preview but I only saw it on Fabric048. TEST SEAMS by looking before use. On the physical size: "dim" is the
  real-world width of one tile in cm (from the API), useful for texel density: 256 px per metre means a 1K tile (1024 px)
  should cover about 4 m, so for cloth you will want the tile to cover about 20 to 60 cm: use the 1K at a UV scale of
  about 2 to 4x of the listed dim at the cost of blur; do the sums with the dim.
- Smallest download: Poly Haven offers 1k, 2k, 4k, 8k; the 1k diffuse JPG sizes are given. ambientCG's smallest is 1K-JPG
  (a zip of all maps, 4 to 11 MB per asset; sizes shown in KB). Both go down only to 1k (1024 px); the team halves in code.
- Both sites' map sets include colour (Diffuse/Color), normal (GL and DX), roughness, AO, displacement; Poly Haven also
  supplies an ARM pack (AO, roughness, metal) which suits the game's packed maps.

## 4. How cloth reads at game distance: a checklist

This list is from standard garment-modelling and character-art practice, not from one citable source. I did not fetch
external cloth-rendering references for it, so it carries no source URL. Test each point on a sheet at 3 m and 20 m.

1. Silhouette first. The outline of the garment must differ from the body: a coat flares, a puffer is rounded, a tee hem
   floats a little off the hip, a trouser falls straight from the knee. If the garment is the body shape, it reads as skin.
2. Break at the joints. At the elbow and knee, add a pinch of 6 to 10 mm: a crease line, a darker fold, a bump in the
   outline. Sleeves bend into an arm, not a tube. Trousers bunch at the knee and stack on the shoe.
3. Hem thickness. Every cut edge shows its fabric thickness: 6 to 15 mm band (jacket 14, hoodie 17, puffer 30 at the
   armpit, per the CLOTHES seat's numbers). A hem as a zero-thickness edge reads as paper. A rolled or folded hem (jeans
   cuff, tee) is a visible 1 cm band lighter or darker than the body.
4. Collar stand. A collar is a ring standing 2.5 to 4 cm off the neck, with a gap and a shadow beneath; a point collar
   has two wedge points; a hood is a thick roll of 8 to 12 cm behind the neck. A flat collar reads as a sticker.
5. Fold placement. Fold where the cloth is pushed: the crotch and the back of the knee on trousers (a fan of creases),
   the armpit and crook of the arm on sleeves, the waist on a tucked shirt, the belly on a leather jacket. Do not put
   random wrinkles everywhere. Stiff fabric: few large folds; limp fabric: many small ones; puffy fabric: horizontal
   pinches between baffles; heavy wool: long vertical folds.
6. Value contrast between layers. A jacket over a sweater over a tee: each at a different brightness (about 0.1 to 0.2
   albedo apart) with a visible shadow where the outer layer ends. Same-value layers merge into one blob at 20 m.
7. Denim fade pattern. Wear follows the body: lighter and whiskered at the lap and hips, honeycomb behind the knees,
   lighter along the thigh seams and the hem, darker at the ankle (dirt), a slightly lighter front pocket outline. Not
   random patches or uniform noise.
8. Knit ribbing direction. Rib runs along the length of the cuff, waistband, collar and hem band (vertical on a hem band,
   along the length of a sleeve end). A stripe running the wrong way reads as a different object.
9. Seams and closures. A centre-front line, a shoulder seam, a side seam in the albedo or a normal map; a visible button
   line or zip. They are free detail that makes a flat shape read as cut cloth.
10. Surface response. Matte jersey, a faint sheen on wool at stress points, a sharp specular on leather and nylon;
    roughness difference is what separates a nylon puffer from a wool coat at the same colour.
11. Worn, not dirty. Dark at the hem and cuff, light at the shoulder, elbow and knee, one place that shines (the seat).
12. Scale of the texture. At 256 px per metre a weave is invisible: do not paint a fine weave; paint the broad value
    pattern (stripe, check, fade) and let the normal map carry the grain. Check pattern size: a plaid repeat of 8 to 12 cm
    at arm's length; a corduroy wale at 3 to 4 mm cannot be seen at 3 m, so show it as stripes at 2 to 3 cm.
